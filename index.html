A SIGNAL IN ACTION

The right next move starts with the right question.

An anonymized positioning example. Rather than guessing which message would work, we started with a specific question and examined what customers were already saying.

THE QUESTION

What should this security product lead with?

The category had several plausible messages: advanced technology, wireless convenience, easy installation and protection.

WHAT WE LOOKED AT
“I needed cameras where there was no Wi-Fi or existing wiring.”
“Installation was the reason this worked for my property.”
“I wanted something I could use in a remote location without building new infrastructure.”

Representative paraphrases of recurring review themes used to illustrate the research method.

THE SIGNAL

The differentiator may not be “better security.”

The more specific story was freedom from infrastructure: surveillance in places where traditional systems become difficult to install or maintain.

QUESTIONS

A few things you might be wondering.

What happens after I complete the questionnaire?

We use your answers to investigate relevant public evidence and prepare preliminary signals. We share what we found, along with uncertainties, before the Mapping Session. These signals are starting points to examine together, not validated conclusions.

Why do you research before the session?

Your client challenge gives us a focused question to investigate. The goal is to arrive with preliminary signals and open questions, so we can use the session to check them against business context and constraints.

Do I need to prepare anything?

Only the short questionnaire. Share the client decision you’re facing and enough context to guide our initial research.

Will you always find customer reviews?

No. Reviews are one useful source when they exist, but the method is broader. We may use public customer comments, testimonials, product pages, competitor claims or other relevant public signals. The source depends on the question.

What are preliminary signals?

Observations from an initial review of relevant evidence. They may point to patterns, tensions or unanswered questions, but they are not confirmed findings. We examine their relevance and limitations together in the Mapping Session.

Why is the Mapping Session free?

Because this first step helps determine whether there is a meaningful opportunity to pursue and whether structured experimentation would actually reduce uncertainty.

Does every Mapping Session lead to a partnership?

No. The Mapping Session helps us understand the client challenge and assess whether continued investigation or experimentation would be useful. A partnership is only considered if it makes sense for both sides.

What if a client does not have enough traffic for A/B testing?

We assess test feasibility and realistic timelines before recommending experiments. Customer research, funnel diagnosis, behavioral analysis, confirmed fixes or monitoring may be better next steps.

Will the first session deliver a complete strategy?

No. Preliminary research helps us begin with evidence; the 30-minute Mapping Session is for understanding the client's context, constraints, assumptions and next questions.

Can our agency apply this with multiple clients?

That is the direction of our future Testing Partnership. Scope, access and delivery terms will be agreed separately; the free session focuses on one challenge.

Do you guarantee conversion uplift?

No. Our approach supports better-informed decisions and honest learning, not guaranteed outcomes or statistical certainty.
